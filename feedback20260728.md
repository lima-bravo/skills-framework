# Skills Framework Card-Gap Review

**Review date:** 2026-07-28  
**Scope:** The 276 canonical cards in `Skills Reference`, including category distribution, card content, connections, references, pre-built chains, and situation mappings.

## Executive summary

The framework is already broad and structurally healthy, but its most consequential gaps are in learning science, decision analysis, externalities, and AI epistemic risk—not in the largest categories.

All 276 canonical cards pass strict structural validation. The count and reference-integrity checks also pass, covering 403 sources and references. The principal opportunities are conceptual balance, a small number of genuinely missing models, and better discoverability of cards that already exist.

## Highest-priority missing cards

| Priority | Proposed card | Why it is genuinely distinct |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Value of Information** | Expected Value evaluates options; Bayesian Updating revises beliefs. This model decides whether further investigation is worth its cost—or whether to act now. |
| 2 | **Counterfactual Reasoning** | Causal Analysis asks why something happened; counterfactual reasoning tests causality by asking what would probably have happened otherwise. It is essential for evaluations, post-mortems, and value realization. |
| 3 | **Externalities** | Interdependence maps connections, while Commons and Free Rider describe special cases. The general model of costs or benefits imposed on third parties is absent. |
| 4 | **Transaction Costs** | Friction appears analogically, but there is no model explaining why firms, contracts, intermediaries, standards, and make/buy boundaries exist. It is highly transferable. |
| 5 | **Desirable Difficulties** | Retrieval practice, spacing, and interleaving are referenced incidentally in Learning Transfer and the training guide. The key insight—that difficulty during practice can improve durable learning—is missing. |
| 6 | **Double-Loop Learning** | PDCA and After-Action Review improve actions inside an existing frame. Double-loop learning asks whether the goals, rules, and assumptions generating those actions are themselves wrong. |
| 7 | **Judgment Noise / Decision Hygiene** | Observer Bias covers systematic expectation effects. Noise concerns inconsistent judgments across people and time. The distinction is acknowledged in Observer Bias but not developed. This could be a new card or a deliberate split/reframing of Observer Bias. |
| 8 | **AI Epistemic Grounding** | The AI collection covers authority, permissions, injection, evaluation, and oversight, but not the basic failure that plausible generated claims are not evidence. A card should cover provenance, citation verification, unsupported synthesis, and the failure of self-verification. |
| 9 | **Chesterton's Fence** | This is the necessary counterweight to Delete Before You Optimise. That card recommends aggressive deletion—including a “10% added back” test—without a corresponding model requiring the original function to be understood first. |

## Strong second-wave candidates

- **Sensitivity Analysis / Decision Robustness** — Identify which assumptions can reverse a decision, rather than merely creating scenarios.

- **Deliberate Practice** — Distinct from Deep Work and the Five-Hour Rule: specific goals, immediate feedback, work at the edge of competence, and correction rather than repetition.

- **Status Quo Bias / Default Effect** — Psychological preference for the current or default option, distinct from structural inertia, path dependence, and sunk cost.

- **Subsidiarity** — Place decisions at the lowest competent level. This complements Mission Command but adds a structural test for decision rights.

- **Switching Costs** — Already used across many cards as an explanatory mechanism but lacks a canonical home.

- **Task vs. Relationship Conflict** — A practical model for preserving useful dissent without allowing disagreement to become personal.

## Cards not recommended yet

Several apparent gaps are already adequately represented:

- **Moral hazard and adverse selection** are explicitly handled inside Principal–Agent Problem.

- **Comparative advantage** is covered by Specialization.

- **Stocks, flows, and leverage points** are covered by Systems Thinking.

- **Distribution shift and automation bias** appear in Algorithms and Human-in-the-Loop Design.

- **Cold-start dynamics** can currently be chained from Network Effects, Critical Mass, and Marketplace.

- **Groupthink** substantially overlaps Psychological Safety, Social Proof, Observer Bias, and Red Teaming. Add it only if recurring situations prove those cards insufficient.

## Collection-level feedback

### 1. Learning is the clearest substantive gap

Learning contains only six cards. Category size alone should not drive additions—the four-card Network Effects category is narrow but coherent—but Learning is thin in substance, not merely count. Several foundational mechanisms of durable learning are absent:

- retrieval practice;
- spacing;
- interleaving;
- desirable difficulties;
- deliberate practice; and
- double-loop learning.

The best initial additions would be Desirable Difficulties and Double-Loop Learning, followed by Deliberate Practice if practitioner situations justify a separate card.

### 2. Clarify the framework's conceptual boundary

The framework philosophy describes a library of transferable mental models rather than domain methods or process frameworks. In practice, the collection now includes operational methods such as DORA Metrics, RACI, Kanban, OKRs, and governance practices.

That is not necessarily wrong, but the collection is now effectively one of:

1. a strict mental-model library; or
2. mental models plus practitioner methods and reusable protocols.

Future additions will be easier to judge once this boundary is explicit. If the broader interpretation is retained, it should be stated in the philosophy and authoring guidance.

### 3. Do not fill categories mechanically

Card count is not a sufficient gap signal. A candidate should still pass the framework's four admission tests:

1. It is a transferable thinking pattern, not a domain fact.
2. It has genuine predictive or diagnostic power.
3. It has a realistic named failure mode.
4. It is distinct from existing cards.

The priority list above is based on those tests rather than an attempt to equalize category sizes.

### 4. Deepen evidence before broad expansion

The manifest is healthy overall, but 24 non-chain cards currently have only one reference. These are concentrated particularly in Innovation & Entrepreneurship, along with a handful of Mathematics, Economics, Psychology, Business, Leadership, Systems, Art, and General Thinking cards.

Before adding a large batch, consider strengthening the most central one-reference cards so that evidence quality grows alongside coverage.

## Discoverability and metadata issues

The situation finder currently contains five stale ID/name mappings:

| Card name in situation finder | Current ID | Correct manifest ID |
|---|---:|---:|
| Reference Class Forecasting | 67 | 160 |
| Planning Fallacy | 143 | 115 |
| Goodhart's Law | 174 | 120 |
| Activation Energy | 21 | 45 |
| Engagement Lifecycle | 165 | 164 |

It also contains ten chain entries with `null` IDs.

Across 64 situations, the finder references 135 distinct card IDs out of 276 cards. Not every card needs a situation mapping, but this suggests that expanding and validating situation coverage may create more practical value than adding lower-priority cards.

These metadata fixes should precede a large card batch so that existing cards do not appear absent merely because they are difficult to discover.

## Recommended first batch

If adding cards in one focused batch, use this sequence:

1. Value of Information
2. Counterfactual Reasoning
3. Externalities
4. Transaction Costs
5. Desirable Difficulties
6. Double-Loop Learning
7. AI Epistemic Grounding
8. Chesterton's Fence

Judgment Noise / Decision Hygiene should follow after deciding whether it deserves a standalone card or whether Observer Bias should be expanded and reframed.

## Suggested next step

For each first-batch candidate, write a short admission note before authoring the full card:

- practitioner situation it addresses;
- closest existing cards;
- the precise distinction from those cards;
- predictive or diagnostic claim;
- likely misuse or failure mode; and
- proposed category and high-signal cluster.

Reject or merge any candidate whose distinction cannot be expressed clearly in two or three sentences. This preserves the framework's quality-over-quantity principle.
