# Market Entry
*Market Entry*

**Category:** [Pre-built Chains](../index.html#pre-built-chains) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../index.html)**

---

## Step 1 — First Principles: What is this market actually?

Define the market by the job-to-be-done, not by the incumbents. Who has this need? How are they currently getting it satisfied? What is the size of the unmet portion? Avoid working from competitor analysis — define the market from the customer's perspective first.

---

## Step 2 — Circle of Competence: Do we have genuine advantage here?

What do we know about this market, customer, or product category that others don't? What capabilities do we bring that are genuinely superior for this market? Be honest about the gap between familiarity with a market and genuine advantage in it.

---

## Step 3 — Monopoly vs. Competition: What kind of market is this?

Is this a competitive market where margins are thin and you need a specific differentiation strategy to survive? Is there a segment where a monopoly position is achievable through differentiation? Map the competitive landscape with specificity about where the white space is.

---

## Step 4 — Second-order Effects: What happens after we enter?

Trace the competitive response: who will respond, how, on what timeline, and with what resources? How does our entry change the market dynamics? Are there regulatory, partner, or channel responses that will affect the viability of the entry strategy?

---

## Step 5 — Customer Development: Have we spoken to enough customers to validate the entry assumptions?

What are the five most dangerous assumptions underlying the entry business case? Have they been tested with real customers? What is the minimum evidence required to proceed with confidence?

---

## Step 6 — Assumption Mapping: Rank and test the critical assumptions.

Map all entry assumptions on importance vs. certainty. The high-importance, low-certainty quadrant defines the tests required before committing significant capital to the entry.

---

## Step 7 — Margin of Safety: What does the stress scenario look like?

Model the entry under worst-credible conditions: customer acquisition takes three times as long, competitor response is faster and better-resourced, the first major customer churns. Is the entry still viable? If not, what changes would make it so?

---

## Step 8 — MVP: What is the minimum entry that generates valid learning?

Design the entry as a structured experiment. What is the cheapest way to test the entry thesis before deploying full capital? A pilot geography, a lighthouse customer, a partnership — whatever generates maximum learning at minimum commitment.
