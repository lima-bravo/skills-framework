# Should I Quit

**Category:** [Pre-built Chains](../../docs/deck.html) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../../docs/deck.html)**

---
## Step 1 — Sunk Cost check: Am I staying because it makes forward sense, or because of what I've invested?

Explicitly acknowledge the investment made (years, relationships, identity, opportunity costs forgone). Then bracket it: "If this were year one, knowing what I know now, would I choose this role/company/endeavor?" The sunk cost should not be a reason to stay; it is done.

---

## Step 2 — Second-order Effects: What happens if I stay vs. if I leave?

Model both directions explicitly. Stay: what does year 3 and year 5 look like on the current trajectory? Leave: what doors open, what doors close, what relationships are affected, what reputation effects occur?

---

## Step 3 — Opportunity Cost: What am I giving up by staying?

The decision to stay is a decision not to pursue alternatives. Name the specific alternatives: other roles, other companies, starting something, returning to a field you left. Are any of these clearly superior to the current situation on a forward-looking basis?

---

## Step 4 — Circle of Competence: Is my unhappiness/dissatisfaction coming from something I'm well-placed to judge?

Some dissatisfaction is meaningful signal (misaligned values, role that doesn't match skills, organization in genuine decline). Some is noise (temporary frustration, normal difficulty of any challenging work, the grass-is-greener illusion). Which is this?

---

## Step 5 — Inversion: What would definitely make this situation work?

Before concluding it doesn't work, be specific: what would need to change for this situation to be one you'd actively choose to be in? If the changes required are realistic (role scope, team composition, reporting structure), have you explicitly requested them? If they are structural to the organization and unlikely to change, that is meaningful information.

---

## Step 6 — Regression to the Mean: Is this a temporary extreme or a stable average?

Is the dissatisfaction coming from an unusually bad period (which will regress) or from the long-run average of the experience? Distinguish temporary lows (high-stress period, difficult project) from chronic systematic issues (values misalignment, poor manager who won't change, structural role fit problem).

---

## Step 7 — Probabilistic Thinking: What is the expected value of each path?

Assign rough probabilities to the scenarios under each option: stay and conditions improve (probability?), stay and conditions stay the same (probability?), leave and land in a clearly superior situation (probability?), leave and land laterally or worse (probability?). The expected value calculation is the frame for the decision.

---

## Step 8 — Margin of Safety: Do I have the financial and personal runway to make this decision well?

Decisions made under acute financial pressure or emotional exhaustion are compromised. Assess your runway — financial, relational, psychological — and make the decision at a moment of adequate stability if possible.
