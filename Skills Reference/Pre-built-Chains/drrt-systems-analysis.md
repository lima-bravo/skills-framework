# DRRT Systems Analysis

**Category:** [Pre-built Chains](../../docs/deck.html) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../../docs/deck.html)**

---
## When to Use

Reach for DRRT when a system is misbehaving and you need to understand its structure before intervening — a recurring operational problem, a process that resists every fix, a team or market behaving in ways no one intended. It is a general-purpose analysis loop: Deconstruct the system, map the Relationships between its parts, Recognise which known structure you are in, and Test the diagnosis before committing. The discipline is sequence — most failed interventions skip straight from a vague symptom to a fix without ever mapping the structure that produces it.

---

## Step 1 — Deconstruct: What is the system actually made of?

Break the system into its elements and draw its boundary — what is in, what is out, what it takes as given. Identify the stocks (what accumulates: backlog, trust, debt, capability) and the flows (what moves between them). Use **First Principles** to strip the system to its irreducible parts rather than its inherited description, and **MECE** to check the decomposition has no gaps or overlaps. If the system is a contested human-activity system — where stakeholders disagree about what it is *for* — run **CATWOE** first to fix its root definition before taking it apart. **Output:** an inventory of parts, stocks, and flows, with an explicit boundary.

---

## Step 2 — Relationships: How do the parts drive each other?

Connect the parts into a wiring diagram. Draw the **Feedback Loops** — reinforcing and balancing — and mark the time **delays**, which are where the non-obvious behaviour hides. Use **Causal Analysis** to trace whether cause and effect run as a chain or a loop, and map the **Interdependencies** and dependencies across elements. The product is not a list of parts but a causal-loop map of how they push each other over time. The rule from the Feedback Loops card holds: most surprising system behaviour comes from a loop or delay that was never on anyone's diagram. **Output:** a causal-loop map with the dominant loops and delays marked.

---

## Step 3 — Recognise: Which known structure is this?

Match the wiring diagram against the **System Archetypes** library. Is this Limits to Growth (a reinforcing engine hitting a constraint), Shifting the Burden (a symptomatic fix eroding the fundamental one), Escalation, Success-to-the-Successful, a Commons tragedy? Recognising the archetype inherits decades of accumulated knowledge: its characteristic leverage point, and the "obvious" intervention that will backfire. If no archetype fits, say so explicitly and treat the structure as novel rather than forcing the nearest match. **Output:** the named archetype (or an explicit "novel structure"), with its leverage point identified.

---

## Step 4 — Test: Does the structure hold, and does the leverage move it?

Treat the recognised structure as a hypothesis, not a verdict. State what it predicts — the behaviour over time this archetype should produce — and check it against the evidence. Then design the smallest intervention that would confirm the leverage point or falsify the diagnosis before committing real resources, using **Falsification** and **Experiment Design** to structure the probe and **PDCA** to run it with a written prediction. If the structure explains the behaviour and the leverage moves it, scale the intervention; if it doesn't, return to Step 1 — the deconstruction or the mapping was wrong. **Output:** a confirmed (or revised) diagnosis and a tested leverage point.

---

**Key failure modes to avoid:** stopping at Deconstruct (a parts list is not a system); mapping the relationships you assume rather than the ones you trace; pattern-matching to an archetype the loops do not actually support; and skipping Test — treating a recognised pattern as proven and intervening at full scale on an unverified diagnosis.

**Decision output:** a named system structure, its leverage point, and a small-scale-tested intervention ready to scale — or an explicit verdict that the structure is novel or unconfirmed and needs further probing before any large commitment.
