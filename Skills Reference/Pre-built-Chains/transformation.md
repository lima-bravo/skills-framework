# Transformation Chain

**Category:** [Pre-built Chains](../../docs/deck.html) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../../docs/deck.html)**

---
## When to Use

Before launching any major organisational transformation: a merger, new operating model, digital transformation, cultural shift, or strategic pivot. If the transformation is prompted by organisational underperformance rather than a strategic opportunity, run the [**Organisational Degradation Scan**](organisational-degradation-scan.md) first — it identifies which degradation mechanisms are operative and ensures the transformation addresses root causes rather than symptoms.

---

## Step 1 — Diagnose Current State

→ **Cynefin Framework · Organizational Culture · Stakeholder Mapping**

The Cynefin Framework gives you the diagnostic ontology: is each component of the transformation operating in a Clear, Complicated, Complex, or Chaotic domain? The domain determines the execution strategy — Clear domains support top-down rollout with best-practice mandates; Complex ones require probe-and-sense, with small experiments and emergent learning. Organisational Culture maps the informal operating system: the norms, rituals, and power dynamics that will either carry or resist the change. These are rarely visible in org charts and rarely discussed in executive briefings, but they are the primary determinant of whether the transformation takes hold. Stakeholder Mapping identifies which actors have the power to stop the transformation, which need to be actively enrolled, and which will experience the change as a threat.

**Output:** A written diagnosis stating the specific problem the transformation addresses, the domain classification of its major components, and a stakeholder map with influence and interest ratings.

---

## Step 2 — Build Urgency and Coalition

→ **Change Management (Kotter Steps 1–2)**

Kotter's first two steps — establishing a sense of urgency and building a guiding coalition — are prerequisites to any planning. A transformation that begins without a credible urgency case fails when the first resistance emerges. Urgency must be factual, not manufactured: the diagnostic evidence from Step 1 and, where applicable, from the Organisational Degradation Scan. The guiding coalition must include people who control the resources the transformation will depend on and the informal opinion leaders whose alignment shapes how the wider organisation reads the change. A sponsor who says yes but does not visibly lead is not a coalition member.

**Output:** A written urgency case grounded in diagnostic evidence, plus a named coalition with confirmed alignment from each member.

---

## Step 3 — Define the Target State

→ **Scenario Planning · Mission Command · Theory of Change**

Scenario Planning stress-tests the target state before committing to it: run at least two plausible adverse scenarios and ask whether the target state remains the right destination in each. Mission Command then translates the target state into a statement of intent — what the organisation must achieve and why, with enough clarity that distributed actors can make aligned decisions independently without requiring central coordination. Theory of Change forces explicit causal reasoning: which interventions are expected to produce which intermediate outcomes, and which outcomes are expected to produce the final result. Without this, the transformation has a destination but no tested route.

**Output:** A one-page transformation intent statement (Mission Command format) plus a Theory of Change diagram specifying the causal chain from current state to target state.

---

## Step 4 — Clarify Governance and Accountability

→ **RACI Framework · Board Governance · Principal–Agent Problem**

The RACI Framework makes decision rights explicit for the most contested choices during the transformation — including decisions about scope, resource reallocation, and stopping criteria. Governance failures in transformation are almost always failures of clarity: multiple people think they are accountable, or no one is. Board Governance defines the authority boundary and escalation path for the transformation sponsor and surfaces which decisions require board visibility versus which can be made at the programme level. The Principal–Agent Problem identifies where the interests of transformation leaders and those executing the work are likely to diverge — and designs reporting structures, incentives, and governance mechanisms to close that gap before it becomes visible as resistance or sandbagging.

**Output:** A governance model documenting decision rights, escalation paths, reporting cadence, and the formal authority of the transformation office.

---

## Step 5 — Manage Risk

→ **Pre-mortem · Risk Management · Margin of Safety**

Run a pre-mortem from the target state: assume the transformation has failed eighteen months from now and ask what caused it. This generates the risk register bottom-up, surfacing failure modes that top-down risk analysis misses. Risk Management then quantifies and prioritises the register — likelihood, impact, velocity, and owner. Margin of Safety converts the top-ranked risks into explicit buffers: timeline slack sized to the schedule risks, budget reserves sized to the cost risks, and scope flexibility designed in before execution begins. Buffers designed after scope is locked are not buffers — they are wishful thinking.

**Output:** A risk register with likelihood/impact ratings, owners, and mitigation actions, plus named contingency buffers in timeline, budget, and scope.

---

## Step 6 — Execute Adaptively

→ **OODA Loop · Capacity Planning · Bottlenecks**

The OODA Loop — Observe, Orient, Decide, Act — is the operating rhythm for adaptive execution. Run compressed OODA cycles at weekly or bi-weekly cadence, updating the picture of current state and re-prioritising workstreams as conditions change. A transformation plan that does not update is a project plan, not a transformation. Capacity Planning ensures the transformation load is viable alongside business-as-usual demands; most transformations underestimate the cost of running both simultaneously with the same people. Bottlenecks identifies the single constraint currently gating transformation progress — the workstream or decision that everything else is waiting on — so attention is directed there rather than spread across all workstreams simultaneously.

**Output:** A running weekly OODA log, a current-state capacity model showing transformation versus BAU load, and an identified bottleneck with a named owner and unblocking action.

---

## Step 7 — Generate and Celebrate Early Wins

→ **Change Management (Kotter Steps 5–6)**

Kotter's fifth and sixth steps — empowering broad-based action and generating short-term wins — maintain the coalition's credibility and signal to the wider organisation that the transformation is real and producing results. Early wins must be genuine, not cosmetic; visible to the skeptics who matter most; and directly connected to the transformation's theory of change so they provide evidence, not just morale. This step is where most transformations quietly stall: execution complexity absorbs leadership attention, and the intentional work of surfacing and celebrating visible progress is treated as optional. It is not optional — it is the mechanism by which the broader organisation updates its belief that the transformation will succeed.

**Output:** A planned win cadence with at least one named, measurable early win in the first ninety days, plus a communication plan for how each win will be made visible.

---

## Step 8 — Institutionalise

→ **Organizational Culture · Goodhart's Law · Feedback Loops**

Institutionalisation converts temporary change into durable operating norm. Organisational Culture is the target: the transformation is complete when the new behaviours are embedded in rituals, artefacts, and the visible conduct of leaders — not when the programme is officially closed. Goodhart's Law is the watchdog: the metrics chosen to track transformation progress will be optimised in ways that diverge from the underlying outcomes they represent; monitor for this and adjust. Feedback Loops designs the permanent sensing mechanism — the regular review, the leading indicator dashboard, the retrospective cadence — that will detect drift from the new state before it becomes a reversion crisis.

**Output:** A maintenance plan specifying the rituals, metrics, and review cadences that will sustain the transformation after the transformation team disbands.
