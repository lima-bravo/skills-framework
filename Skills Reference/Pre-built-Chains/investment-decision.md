# Investment Decision
*Investment Decision*

**Category:** [Pre-built Chains](../../docs/deck.html) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../../docs/deck.html)**

---

## Step 1 — First Principles: What is the intrinsic value?

Before looking at the price, establish a view on value. What cash flows will this asset produce, over what time horizon, at what level of certainty? What is the replacement cost? What is the liquidation value in the downside scenario? Build your value estimate independently before exposing it to the asking price.

---

## Step 2 — Circle of Competence: Do I understand this business well enough to underwrite it?

What is my genuine edge in evaluating this investment? If the answer is "general business judgment and the information in the deck," that is not a circle of competence for a specific investment. Identify specifically what you know that would allow you to evaluate this accurately.

---

## Step 3 — Probabilistic Thinking: What is the distribution of outcomes?

Build an explicit scenario analysis: base case (most likely), bull case (things go well), bear case (adverse but plausible), and catastrophic case (worst credible scenario). Assign probabilities and expected values to each. The expected value of the investment is the probability-weighted average of these scenarios.

---

## Step 4 — Second-order Effects: What happens after this investment is made?

If this investment is successful, who else enters the market? What does success do to the competitive landscape, the regulatory environment, and the unit economics? Trace the second-order effects on the investment thesis.

---

## Step 5 — Margin of Safety: Is there a sufficient gap between price and value?

Even if the value estimate is correct, the investment only generates adequate returns if the price provides sufficient discount. The gap between price and intrinsic value is the margin of safety — the protection against estimation error and adverse outcomes.

---

## Step 6 — Sunk Cost check: Am I making this decision freshly?

If you have previously invested in this opportunity (prior investment, time spent, emotional commitment), run an explicit sunk cost check: "If I were encountering this investment for the first time today, would I invest?" If the answer is no, the sunk cost is contaminating the decision.

---

## Step 7 — Inversion: What would make this investment fail?

List the specific conditions that would produce a catastrophic return. Are these conditions identifiable in advance? Are there monitoring signals? What is your exit condition if these conditions begin to manifest?

---

## Step 8 — Incentives: Are the principals aligned with my interests?

Management/founder incentives, fund structure incentives, and board incentives all shape whether the people responsible for this investment are optimizing for the same outcome you are. Misaligned incentives produce systematic underperformance relative to potential.
