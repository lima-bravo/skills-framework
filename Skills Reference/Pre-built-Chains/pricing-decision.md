# Pricing Decision

**Category:** [Pre-built Chains](../../docs/deck.html) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../../docs/deck.html)**

---
## Step 1 — First Principles: What are we actually selling?

Strip away existing pricing conventions in your category. Ask: what value does the customer actually receive, and what would they pay to get that value from their best alternative (time, other products, doing without)?

---

## Step 2 — Customer Development: What is the customer's willingness to pay based on evidence?

Run value-anchored pricing interviews using the Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter or direct willingness-to-pay questions with existing customers. Distinguish the price at which the product feels too cheap (quality concern), too expensive (rejection), expensive-but-acceptable, and a good deal. Do not rely on stated willingness to pay alone — test with real payment gates.

---

## Step 3 — Trade-offs: What does each pricing model trade off?

Map the decision: per-seat pricing (predictable revenue, scales with customer growth, penalises large teams), usage-based (aligned with value, unpredictable, benefits small users), annual prepaid (cash flow positive, requires strong trust), freemium (acquisition efficiency, conversion dependency). Each trade-off has second-order implications for the customer relationship, the sales motion, and cash flow.

---

## Step 4 — Second-order Effects: How does this price signal quality and position the product?

Price is communication. A price below the psychological threshold for the segment signals low quality regardless of product quality. A price above the anchor point for the category signals premium and attracts customers for whom price is a quality signal. Trace the second-order positioning effects of each price point.

---

## Step 5 — Incentives: What behavior does this pricing structure incentivize in the customer?

Usage-based pricing incentivizes customers to minimize usage. Per-seat pricing incentivizes adding users. Annual contracts incentivize low switching costs after the contract ends. Design the pricing structure so the incentivized behavior aligns with the customer's desired outcome.

---

## Step 6 — Experiment Design: Run a pricing test before committing.

If possible, test multiple price points with new prospects simultaneously. Define the metric (conversion rate, ACV, NPS at 30 days) and the sample size required to detect a meaningful difference. Don't test by asking — test by charging.

---

## Step 7 — Margin of Safety: Can you sustain at this price under adverse scenarios?

At the lowest credible volume, does this price cover fully loaded cost of delivery with adequate margin? Model the stress scenario before committing.

**Key failure modes to avoid:** Anchoring to your cost structure rather than customer value; underpricing out of fear rather than evidence; setting price that contradicts positioning.
