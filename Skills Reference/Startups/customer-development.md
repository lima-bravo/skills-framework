# Customer Development

**Category:** [Innovation & Entrepreneurship](../../docs/deck.html) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../../docs/deck.html)**

---
## Definition

Customer development, developed by Steve Blank, is the process of systematically testing business model assumptions through direct customer contact before, during, and after product development. The insight: most startups fail not because they can't build the product, but because they build the wrong product for the wrong customer with the wrong business model. Customer development replaces assumptions about customers with direct evidence.

Blank's four steps: Customer Discovery (hypothesize and test the problem and solution), Customer Validation (test whether you can sell the product), Customer Creation (build the sales and marketing machine), and Company Building (scale what's validated).

---

## Mental Model

The practitioner treats every assumption about customers — their problem, their needs, their willingness to pay, their buying process — as a falsifiable hypothesis that requires evidence, not intuition. The customer is not a person to sell to; they are an evidence source. The question is not "will you buy this?" (leading, generates false positives) but "tell me about the last time you experienced [problem]" (open, generates genuine evidence).

---

## Practitioner Heuristics

- **Get out of the building.** The central principle. No amount of internal analysis, competitor research, or desk research substitutes for direct customer contact. The assumptions that kill companies are most often the ones no one tested because no one spoke to customers.
- **Distinguish problem interviews from solution interviews.** In early customer discovery, talk only about the problem — don't mention your solution. The goal is to understand the problem with perfect fidelity before designing any solution. Mentioning the solution prematurely activates social desirability bias.
- **Listen for "I've tried to solve this" evidence.** Customers who have actively tried to solve the problem (built workarounds, paid for imperfect alternatives, searched extensively) are experiencing genuine pain. Customers who "might find it useful" are not your early adopters.
- **Use "the mom test" (Rob Fitzpatrick).** Customers lie to be polite. Design your questions so that honest answers are socially safe: ask about past behavior (what they have done) rather than future intention (what they would do), ask about cost/pain (what they've already spent on solutions), and ask who else has the problem.
- **Look for the customer who is already solving the problem imperfectly.** The person hacking together a solution from spreadsheets and duct tape is your beachhead customer.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Validation theater.** Running interviews with leading questions, hearing what you want to hear, and declaring customer validation complete. This is confirmation bias operationalized as a process.
- **Talking only to early adopters.** Early adopters have different needs, tolerance for friction, and willingness to pay than mainstream customers. Validate with mainstream customers before scaling.
- **Conflating customer development with sales.** Customer development conversations are research; treating them as sales opportunities corrupts the data and damages relationships.

---

## Worked Example

The founders of Airbnb validated their core hypothesis by manually creating listings for their own apartment and hosting guests themselves. They then went to other Airbnb hosts' apartments personally, helped them photograph their spaces, and asked detailed questions about their hosting motivations, concerns, and pricing logic. This direct evidence — not surveying or desk research — produced the insight that professional photography dramatically increased bookings and that host trust concerns were the primary barrier to listing. Both insights came directly from customer development conversations, not from analysis.
