# Innovation Accounting

**Category:** [Innovation & Entrepreneurship](../../docs/deck.html) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../../docs/deck.html)**

---
## Definition

Innovation accounting (Eric Ries) is a framework for measuring the progress of a startup in a way that is meaningful when traditional metrics (revenue, profit, growth) are not yet established or are misleading. Traditional accounting measures the output of a business operating in a known market. Innovation accounting measures progress toward finding a validated business model — the learning that occurs before the business model is known.

The three milestones of innovation accounting: (1) use an MVP to establish real baseline data; (2) tune the engine — test improvements toward the ideal; (3) pivot or persevere based on whether the trajectory is improving fast enough.

---

## Mental Model

In a startup, improving traditional metrics can actually obscure lack of progress — revenue growth through discounting, or user growth through paid channels, can mask the absence of product-market fit. Innovation accounting separates "working harder on the same model" from "validating the model." The practitioner measures what genuine progress looks like for the stage of the business.

---

## Practitioner Heuristics

- **Define the metrics for each stage of your funnel explicitly.** Awareness → interest → consideration → trial → retention → referral. Which stage is the bottleneck? That stage's metric is the innovation accounting metric to move.
- **Use cohort analysis rather than aggregate metrics.** Aggregate metrics smooth over the difference between old customers (who have been retained through inertia) and new customers (who reflect current product-market fit). Cohort retention curves are the most honest PMF measurement.
- **Treat the vanity metric baseline as the enemy.** Total users, total revenue, total downloads are easy to make look good by adding inputs. Cohort retention, activation rate, and referral rate are harder to game and more informative.
- **Connect every initiative to a hypothesis and an innovation accounting metric.** "We're building Feature X" is not an innovation accounting statement. "We're building Feature X to test whether it moves activation from 20% to 35%, which our model says is the threshold for sustainable growth" is.
- **Run the numbers on "is this trajectory sufficient?"** Even a positive trajectory on the right metrics may not be sufficient. A company improving retention by 1 percentage point per quarter may never reach the threshold for organic growth.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Measuring activity instead of learning.** "We ran 15 experiments" is activity; "we learned that our activation problem is in the onboarding email, not the product" is learning.
- **One-size-fits-all metrics across growth stages.** Pre-PMF metrics (does this work at all?) are different from post-PMF metrics (can we scale this?). Using scale metrics before PMF is confirmed produces misleading conclusions.

---

## Worked Example

A mobile app team tracks DAU (daily active users) as their north star metric. DAU is growing 20% per month — excellent by any standard. But a cohort analysis reveals that day-30 retention is 3% — almost no one who downloads the app is still using it a month later. The growth in DAU is entirely driven by increasing acquisition spend; the product is not retaining users. Innovation accounting reveals the business is not progressing; traditional accounting (revenue, downloads) shows impressive growth.
