# Engagement Lifecycle
*Diagnose — Design — Deliver — Detach*

**Category:** [Consulting Craft](../../docs/deck.html) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../../docs/deck.html)**

---

## Definition

The engagement lifecycle is the explicit shape of a consulting engagement: **Diagnose** (understand the problem and the system), **Design** (propose interventions and align stakeholders), **Deliver** (execute, monitor, adapt), and **Detach** (transfer ownership and exit). Distinguishing these phases — and being honest about which phase the engagement is in — prevents two common failures: rushing to design before diagnosis is honest, and lingering past the appropriate detach. The framing draws on Peter Block's *Flawless Consulting* (1981) and Edgar Schein's *Process Consultation* (1969), updated for modern delivery work.

---

## Mental Model

A house renovation has the same phases: survey (diagnose), design and permitting (design), construction (deliver), and handover with warranty (detach). Confusing them is a recipe for waste and rework. A homeowner who insists on construction before design produces a house no one wants; a contractor who lingers past handover under-charges and degrades trust. Consulting engagements behave similarly — and most engagement pathologies trace to phase-mismatch: leadership wants delivery while diagnosis is open; consultants stay in design because it's intellectually rewarding; nobody plans detach until the budget is depleted.

---

## Practitioner Heuristics

- **Name the current phase explicitly.** Open every steering meeting with: "we are in [phase] for [scope]." Phase confusion is the most common engagement disease.
- **Resist premature design.** A client pushing for "the answer" before diagnosis is honest needs you to hold the line, not to comply.
- **Design with the people who will deliver.** Designs handed off to a separate execution team mostly fail; co-design with execution lifts both quality and adoption.
- **Build the detach into the deliver phase.** Capability transfer, runbooks, and successor identification start at the beginning of delivery, not the end.
- **Audit your hours by phase.** A diagnosis phase consuming 60% of total hours is a sign of either over-investigation or unclear scope.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Diagnosis bypass.** Starting an engagement at "implementation" — usually because the client has decided what they want — produces solutions to the wrong problem.
- **Design without alignment.** A great design that hasn't aligned the stakeholders who must adopt it is shelfware.
- **Endless deliver.** Engagements that lose sight of detach become embedded staff augmentation, eroding the client's capability and the consultant's edge.
- **Detach without transfer.** Walking away cleanly with no successor designated produces regressions within months.

---

## Worked Example

A 9-month transformation engagement for an insurer. Diagnose (weeks 1–6): value-stream mapping, interview wave, baseline metrics. Design (weeks 7–12): three intervention designs, with the client team co-leading. Deliver (weeks 13–32): pilots, scaling, course corrections; capability-transfer plan kicks off at week 20 with a named successor on the client team. Detach (weeks 33–36): final readouts, knowledge transfer, gradual reduction of consultant hours from 4 days per week to 1, then to zero. Six months post-engagement, the client team continues the improvement program without consultant support; metrics continue to trend in the right direction. Compare with the alternative — a no-detach engagement extends for another 18 months, the client team never builds the capability, and the consultancy is fired in year 3 when the budget tightens.
