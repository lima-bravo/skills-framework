# Defensibility
*The ability to maintain competitive advantage*

**Category:** [Network Effects](../index.html#network-effects) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../index.html)**

---

## Definition

Defensibility is the degree to which a competitive position can be maintained against competitive attack. A defensible position has one or more "moats" — structural advantages that raise the cost or lower the effectiveness of competitor attacks. Warren Buffett's framing: he seeks companies with durable economic moats — features of the business model that protect above-average returns from competitive erosion over long periods.

---

## Mental Model

Competitive advantage without defensibility is a price-to-earnings premium that decays back to the industry average as competitors attack. Defensibility converts a temporary advantage into a durable one. The practitioner's question: "In three years, why will this advantage still exist despite rational competitive response?" If the answer is unclear, the advantage is not defensible.

---

## Practitioner Heuristics

- **Identify your moat type and its durability.** Common moats: network effects (durable), switching costs (durable), scale advantages (durable above minimum scale), proprietary data or technology (decays as competitors invest), brand (durable if maintained), regulatory/geographic exclusivity (durable but fragile to regulatory change). Know which type you have and its specific vulnerabilities.
- **Build multiple moats simultaneously.** Single-moat businesses are vulnerable to direct attacks on that one moat. Multiple overlapping moats — brand + switching costs + data + network effects — create a much more complex attack surface.
- **Defensibility must be actively maintained.** Moats are not permanent assets; they are ongoing investments. A network effect moat requires continuous product investment to keep the network engaged. A brand moat requires consistent customer experience. A technology moat requires R&D.
- **Test defensibility through competitive scenario planning.** Ask: if the most well-resourced competitor in our market focused exclusively on beating us for three years, what would happen? The honest answer to this question identifies your actual moat.
- **Beware defensibility that depends on secrecy.** Proprietary technology is a moat; technology that is secret but not genuinely superior (the secret is: "we exist") is not.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Conflating current market position with defensibility.** Being the market leader today does not mean you are defensible. The market leader in every business Airbnb disrupted was defensible by any traditional analysis.
- **Moat erosion through neglect.** Assuming the moat is permanent and stopping the maintenance investment. Brand moats erode through inconsistent experience; network effect moats erode through product stagnation.

---

## Worked Example

Amazon's defensibility is often described as its logistics network. But the actual moat is multi-layered: third-party marketplace network effects (more sellers → more selection → more buyers), Prime's switching costs (annual fee creates sunk cost and habit), AWS (completely separate business generating cash to fund retail subsidies), and proprietary data on customer purchasing behavior. No single competitor can attack all four moats simultaneously. Replicating any one of them requires enormous capital and time; replicating all four is impractical.
