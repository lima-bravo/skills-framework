# Innovation Funnel

**Category:** [Innovation & Entrepreneurship](../../docs/deck.html) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../../docs/deck.html)**

---
## Definition

The innovation funnel is a staged process for managing ideas from initial generation through progressive filtering, testing, and scaling. Ideas enter at the wide top; through successive stages of testing and validation, the successful ones progress toward deployment; the funnel narrows as ideas are filtered on evidence of viability, feasibility, and strategic fit. The funnel manages the balance between generating enough raw ideas (divergence) and rigorously selecting the best ones (convergence).

---

## Mental Model

The funnel is a portfolio of bets at different stages of validation. Early stages carry many small, cheap bets (low evidence, low investment). Later stages carry fewer, larger bets (higher evidence, higher investment). The practitioner's discipline is sizing each bet appropriately to its validation stage and not allowing attachment to specific ideas to override the evidence-based filtering process.

---

## Practitioner Heuristics

- **Define the stage-gate criteria explicitly before the funnel is loaded.** What evidence is required to progress from stage 2 to stage 3? If this isn't defined in advance, the criteria will be applied inconsistently and political considerations will dominate.
- **Separate idea generation from idea evaluation.** Simultaneous generation and evaluation suppresses idea generation (critical evaluation kills nascent ideas). Run divergence phases before convergence phases.
- **Maintain a portfolio across stages.** An organization with only late-stage bets (developed products close to deployment) has no pipeline. An organization with only early-stage bets (ideas) has no results. The funnel should have active ideas at every stage.
- **Kill early, cheaply.** The purpose of early-stage validation is to identify non-starters before significant investment. The metric for early stages: speed to kill, not quality of the killed idea. The faster you eliminate non-viable ideas, the more resources remain for viable ones.
- **Connect the funnel to strategic intent.** Innovation funnels that are disconnected from strategy produce interesting ideas that don't serve the organization's direction. The filter at each stage should include strategic fit.
- **Choose stage-gate when investment jumps are large and reversals are costly; choose flow-based governance when investment is incremental.** Stage-gate requires formal approval at each transition; flow-based governance (continuous funding, regular outcome reviews) suits work where learning should trigger re-direction, not a formal review.
- **Default to a hybrid, not a pure form of either.** Pure stage-gate creates batch inventory and delays feedback; pure flow lets bad bets go un-killed because no formal review ever forces the question. Run light-touch flow within a stage, and reserve formal gates for the transitions that carry the largest step-changes in commitment.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Zombie ideas.** Ideas that aren't progressed and aren't killed — they persist in the funnel consuming mindshare and conversation without generating learning. Stage-gate discipline with real kill criteria prevents zombies.
- **Funnel without a sponsor.** Ideas that progress through the funnel without an internal champion who will advocate for resources will stall in late stages. Every idea in the funnel should have an owner.

---

## Worked Example

A FMCG company runs an annual innovation sprint: 200 ideas entered, 50 developed to concept, 20 consumer tested, 5 piloted, 2 launched at scale. The funnel shape is maintained year-over-year. The stage-gate criteria are published and consistently applied. The failure mode they fixed: previously, pet ideas of senior leaders bypassed the funnel and entered late stages without early-stage validation. Two high-profile failures led to a rule: no idea can skip a funnel stage regardless of sponsor seniority.
