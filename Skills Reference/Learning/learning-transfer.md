# Learning Transfer
*Apply insights from one domain to another*

**Category:** [Learning](../index.html#learning) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../index.html)**

---

## Definition

Learning transfer is the application of knowledge, skills, or principles from one context to a different context — particularly across domains. Near transfer is applying learning to similar situations. Far transfer is applying learning to structurally different situations — the genuinely difficult and valuable kind. Most formal learning produces near transfer; it is far transfer that generates the "unexpected connections" and lateral insights that drive creative problem-solving and model chaining.

---

## Mental Model

Every domain is a specialized vocabulary for describing patterns that may also appear in other domains under different names. The practitioner who has studied evolutionary biology recognizes variation-selection-retention dynamics in markets, organizations, and cultural evolution. The one who has studied physics recognizes leverage and inertia dynamics in management. The knowledge did not "apply" narrowly to the original domain; it transferred to illuminate analogous structures elsewhere.

---

## Practitioner Heuristics

- **Learn principles, not just procedures.** Procedures are context-specific; principles transfer. "This is how you set up a Django server" does not transfer; "dependency injection enables testability" transfers across programming paradigms.
- **Abstract the insight before transferring.** The transfer step requires identifying the underlying principle (not the surface feature) of what you learned, then asking where that principle appears in other contexts. "What is this an example of?" is the abstraction question.
- **Cultivate a habit of asking "where else does this apply?"** When encountering an insight, systematically ask which other domains it might illuminate. This is the active practice of far transfer.
- **Build a shared vocabulary across domains.** When you can describe insights from ecology, physics, and economics in the same abstract language (feedback loops, equilibrium, phase transitions), transfer becomes much easier.
- **Test the transfer.** A transferred insight is a hypothesis. Test whether the principle actually operates in the new domain, rather than assuming the analogy is complete.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Superficial analogy.** Two situations that look similar on the surface but are structurally different. Applying the wrong model because the surface features match. Transfer requires structural similarity, not surface similarity.
- **Transfer without adaptation.** Applying a principle from one domain to another without adjusting for the structural differences. The principle may be right; the specific mechanism may not transfer directly.
- **Under-transfer due to domain attachment.** The expert who has spent years in one domain struggles to see that their expertise applies elsewhere — the domain vocabulary is so embedded that the underlying principles are invisible.

---

## Worked Example

A former military officer joins a startup as COO. Her military training: clear mission objectives, tight feedback loops on mission performance, pre-mortem planning (rehearsal of contingencies), and clear command-and-control decision structures. Transferring directly: most of this doesn't work in a startup where autonomy, ambiguity, and cultural norms differ radically from military hierarchy. Transferring the principles: clear objectives work universally (OKRs); tight feedback loops work; pre-mortem planning (reframed as pre-mortems in product, not security briefings) works. The surface mechanism (military structure) didn't transfer; the underlying principles transferred with adaptation.
