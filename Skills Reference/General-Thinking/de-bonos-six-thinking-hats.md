# De Bono's Six Thinking Hats
*Separate modes of thinking to think better together*

**Category:** [General Thinking](../../docs/deck.html) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../../docs/deck.html)**

---

## Definition

Edward de Bono's Six Thinking Hats is a parallel thinking framework that separates different modes of thinking into six distinct "hats" — each representing a single thinking mode that the group adopts simultaneously. White Hat: facts and data only. Red Hat: emotions and intuitions, no justification required. Black Hat: caution, risks, why it won't work. Yellow Hat: optimism, benefits, why it will work. Green Hat: creative alternatives and new possibilities. Blue Hat: process meta-cognition, managing the thinking itself. By wearing one hat at a time, groups escape the adversarial dynamic of debate (where individuals defend positions) and enable parallel thinking (where everyone explores the same dimension together).

---

## Mental Model

In conventional debate, different people are metaphorically wearing different hats simultaneously — one person is being cautious (Black), another is enthusiastic (Yellow), another is proposing alternatives (Green) — and they are arguing against each other. The Six Hats reframes this: everyone wears the same hat at the same time, exploring each dimension collectively. The intellectual richness comes from each hat's perspective; the efficiency comes from the elimination of ego-defense dynamics.

---

## Practitioner Heuristics

- **Use it when group thinking is getting stuck in adversarial loops.** The Six Hats are most valuable not as a routine meeting tool but as an intervention when a discussion has become polarized or when one hat is dominating at the expense of others.
- **Protect the Red Hat.** The most underused in professional contexts: giving explicit permission to name emotions and intuitions without justification. "I have a bad feeling about this" is a valuable signal; the Red Hat provides the space to surface it without requiring rationalization.
- **Use Black Hat and Yellow Hat together.** Running risk analysis (Black) without benefit analysis (Yellow) produces paralysis; running benefit analysis without risk analysis produces naivety. Pair them in sequence.
- **The Blue Hat is leadership's hat.** Managing which hat the group is wearing, for how long, and in what sequence is a facilitation function. Naming the current hat and the intended sequence improves meeting quality significantly.
- **Short hat sessions are most productive.** Three to five minutes in each hat is often sufficient to extract the most useful thinking. Longer sessions encourage repetition rather than depth.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Treating it as a checklist.** Running through all six hats because it's the process, rather than selecting the hats most relevant to the decision. Not every decision needs all six.
- **Black Hat dominance in conservative cultures.** In organizations that reward caution, the Black Hat discussion absorbs most of the meeting time, and Yellow, Red, and Green are rushed. The facilitator must actively protect the other hats.
- **Conflating hats.** Slipping from Yellow (benefits) into advocacy (argue for the option) or from Black (caution) into the Red (emotional opposition). Remind participants of each hat's specific mode.

## Worked Example

A leadership team is deciding whether to acquire a competitor. The discussion has become heated — one camp strongly in favor, one strongly opposed, both talking past each other. A facilitator introduces the Six Hats. White: what do we factually know about their financials, customer base, and technology? (15 minutes) Red: what are our honest gut reactions? (5 minutes, undefended) Yellow: what is the best case? (10 minutes) Black: what are the specific risks? (10 minutes) Green: are there alternative deal structures we haven't considered? (10 minutes) Blue: given all this, what are our next steps? (5 minutes). The same information that was generating conflict in debate mode produces a much richer analysis in parallel thinking mode. The acquisition thesis is refined (not abandoned or approved) based on the structured exploration.
