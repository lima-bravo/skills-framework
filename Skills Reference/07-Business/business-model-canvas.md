# Business Model Canvas
*Map the structure of value creation and capture*

**Category:** [Business](../index.html#business) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../index.html)**

---

## Definition

The Business Model Canvas (Alexander Osterwalder) is a strategic template for developing or documenting a business model. It organizes nine building blocks onto a single page: Customer Segments, Value Propositions, Channels, Customer Relationships, Revenue Streams, Key Resources, Key Activities, Key Partnerships, and Cost Structure. The power of the canvas is forcing explicit choices about every element of the business and making the interdependencies between elements visible.

---

## Mental Model

The canvas is a hypothesis map for the entire business, not just the product. The right side describes how value is delivered to customers (Segments, Value Proposition, Channels, Relationships, Revenue). The left side describes how value is created (Resources, Activities, Partnerships, Costs). A viable business model is one where the right side generates revenue that exceeds the left side's costs, and where the left side's capabilities are genuinely suited to deliver the right side's promises.

---

## Practitioner Heuristics

- **Fill it in as hypotheses, not facts.** In early-stage work, every box is a hypothesis. Label each box with a confidence level. The low-confidence hypotheses are your highest-priority tests.
- **Test the interdependencies, not just the boxes.** The key insight often emerges at the interface between boxes. Does the channel choice match the customer segment's buying behavior? Do the key resources enable the key activities? Does the revenue stream match the value proposition?
- **Use it for competitive analysis.** Map a competitor's canvas alongside yours. Where are the differences? Those differences are the competing hypotheses about how the market works.
- **Iterate the canvas before iterating the product.** Many product problems are actually business model problems. Adding features that don't change customer economics, channel efficiency, or unit economics doesn't improve the model.
- **The value proposition is the center.** If the value proposition is wrong — if it doesn't solve a genuine, important problem for the target segment — no amount of optimization on the other boxes will produce a viable business.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Treating the canvas as a document rather than a tool.** Filling it in once and filing it away is not the intended use. The canvas should be updated every time a significant assumption is tested.
- **Describing the current business rather than questioning it.** The canvas should be provocative: why this customer segment and not another? Why this channel? These are not rhetorical questions.

---

## Worked Example

A founder building a B2B SaaS product fills in the canvas. Under Revenue Streams, she writes "subscription." Under Customer Relationships, she writes "self-serve." The interdependency check: are her Customer Segments (enterprise companies with long procurement cycles) actually compatible with self-serve, low-touch relationships? The canvas exposes the mismatch: enterprise customers need high-touch relationships, not self-serve. This changes both the Revenue Streams box (higher ACV, longer contracts) and the Key Activities box (sales, customer success). One canvas review saved six months of building the wrong go-to-market.
