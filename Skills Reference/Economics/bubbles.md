# Bubbles
*When price detaches from value*

**Category:** [Economics](../index.html#economics) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../index.html)**

---

## Definition

An economic bubble is a period during which asset prices rise significantly above intrinsic value, sustained by investor expectations of continued price appreciation rather than underlying cash flow or utility. Bubbles are amplified by credit, narrative, and social dynamics; they end when the price-value gap becomes impossible to sustain and prices revert, often sharply. The defining characteristic: the price becomes self-referentially justified by the expectation that prices will continue to rise — a circular logic that eventually collapses under its own weight.

---

## Mental Model

Bubbles have a recognisable structure: displacement (a genuine innovation creates legitimate new opportunity), boom (credit and capital flow in, prices rise), euphoria (new valuation frameworks justify ever-higher prices, sceptics are marginalized), profit-taking (sophisticated investors quietly exit), panic, and crash. The practitioner's challenge: at what stage is the market currently in, and does participation make sense at this point? The error is confusing "prices are rizing" with "prices are correct."

---

## Practitioner Heuristics

- **Test valuation against intrinsic value, not relative value.** "It's cheaper than X" is a relative valuation; if X is also overvalued, the comparison is useless. Anchor to discounted cash flows, replacement cost, or fundamental utility.
- **Identify the narrative that's justifying the price.** Every bubble has a "this time is different" narrative. The narrative is usually partially true (the underlying innovation is real) and fatally overstated (the value of the innovation is priced in at multiples that cannot be justified).
- **Watch credit conditions.** Bubbles are almost always credit-fuelled. When previously unavailable capital flows into an asset class (leveraged buyouts for a sector, mortgage-backed securities, crypto lending), the likelihood of bubble dynamics increases.
- **Track sentiment indicators as second-order signals.** Cab drivers and social media influencers discussing the investment, widespread mainstream media coverage of gains, and friend-group investment discussions are historically reliable late-cycle signals.
- **Size positions for participation without fatal exposure.** In a bubble environment, the rational response is not necessarily to exit — bubbles can run far longer than fundamental analysis suggests they should. The practitioner takes reduced exposure that allows participation without catastrophic downside.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Early short.** Correctly identifying a bubble and shorting it before the market reaches the exhaustion phase. Correct in the long run; catastrophic in the short run. Bubbles outlast solvency.
- **Confusing bubble participation with endorsement.** You can participate in a bubble with eyes open (sized appropriately, with an exit trigger) without believing the valuation is justified.
- **Survivorship bias in bubble analysis.** We study the failed bubbles; we forget the genuine new paradigms that initially looked like bubbles (internet, mobile). Distinguishing real from false requires fundamental analysis of the underlying, not just the price dynamics.

---

## Worked Example

The 2000-2001 dot-com bubble followed the classic structure. The displacement: the internet was genuinely transformational. The euphoria: revenue multiples of 40x on firms with no path to profitability, justified by "eyeball economics" and "first mover advantage." The tell: valuation frameworks were invented to justify prices rather than derived from fundamentals. The collapse: 78% drawdown in the NASDAQ from peak to trough. The lesson: the internet was genuinely important (Amazon, Google survived and thrived), but the price paid for "internet exposure" embedded years of future returns at zero, and for most companies, returned nothing.
