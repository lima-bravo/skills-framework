# Gemba
*Go and see — the work, where it happens*

**Category:** [Delivery & Flow](../index.html#delivery-flow) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../index.html)**

---

## Definition

Gemba (現場) is Japanese for "the actual place" — the place where value is created. In lean practice, a "gemba walk" is the discipline of leaders going to the work, observing it directly, and asking questions rather than reading reports about it. The practice was central to Taiichi Ohno's coaching at Toyota and is canonized in Masaaki Imai's *Gemba Kaizen* (1997). The discipline counters two pathologies of seniority: relying on filtered reports, and reasoning about systems from inside conference rooms.

---

## Mental Model

A doctor who has stopped seeing patients can still read journals, attend meetings, and run a hospital — but loses the ability to know what is actually happening. The same dynamic afflicts senior managers in any operational domain. Gemba is the explicit practice of returning to the work to recalibrate. The shock of what a leader observes during a gemba walk — a process that looks one way in a slide deck looks entirely different on the floor — is itself the value. Consultants are particularly vulnerable to map–territory errors; gemba is the antidote.

---

## Practitioner Heuristics

- **Observe before you ask, ask before you advise.** The point is to learn what the work is, not to inspect compliance.
- **Walk with humility.** Workers know more about the work than you do; the questions are for your learning, not to test theirs.
- **Trace one unit, end to end.** Follow one item through the value stream from trigger to delivery. The handoffs and waits will surprise you.
- **Note what you couldn't have predicted.** The whole point is to discover gaps between mental model and reality. Catalogue them.
- **Make it a habit, not an audit.** Routine gemba walks produce trust and learning; one-off "executive visits" produce theater and resentment.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Gemba as inspection.** Walking with a clipboard and asking "why aren't you following the standard?" destroys the practice's value.
- **Gemba as photo opportunity.** Visiting once for optics, never returning, signals everything wrong.
- **Gemba without changes.** If observations don't lead to support, escalation removal, or coaching, workers learn it is performance art.
- **Skipping gemba for digital work.** "The work" for knowledge workers is in tickets, chat, code, and meetings. Digital gemba is harder to do but no less necessary.

---

## Worked Example

A consulting partner overseeing a transformation reads weekly status reports indicating smooth progress. A two-hour gemba — sitting in a stand-up, observing a handoff between teams, sitting with one engineer through a deployment — reveals: a critical handoff is being handled via personal Slack DMs, one engineer is doing 60% of the load and showing burnout, and the "automated" deployment requires three manual approvals nobody mentioned in the report. None of these would have surfaced in another quarter of status meetings. The follow-up: a redesigned handoff (eliminating the DM channel), a reassignment of load, and removal of two of the three manual gates. The partner now walks every value stream once a quarter.
