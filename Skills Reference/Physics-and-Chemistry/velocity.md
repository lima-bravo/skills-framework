# Velocity
*Speed and direction together*

**Category:** [Physics & Chemistry](../../docs/deck.html) &nbsp;|&nbsp; **[← Card Deck](../../docs/deck.html)**

---

## Definition

Velocity is a vector quantity — it has both magnitude (speed) and direction. In physics, this distinguishes it from speed, which has only magnitude. A car moving 60mph north and a car moving 60mph south have the same speed but opposite velocities. The practitioner's application: high throughput in the wrong direction is not progress — it is rapid movement away from the goal. Effective velocity requires both speed and directional alignment.

---

## Mental Model

The practitioner's question is never just "how fast are we going?" but always "how fast, and in what direction, toward what goal?" A team that ships features rapidly but ships features that don't move the needle is operating at high speed but low velocity. Directional clarity — knowing which outcomes you're pursuing and why — is a prerequisite for velocity to be meaningful.

---

## Practitioner Heuristics

- **Establish directional clarity before optimizing speed.** Velocity without direction is just activity. Spend time on the "which direction" question proportional to the cost of heading the wrong way at high speed.
- **Measure velocity as progress toward the goal, not output generated.** Engineering velocity measured in story points is speed; engineering velocity measured in learning or validated customer outcomes is directional velocity.
- **Identify when you are in a velocity phase vs. a direction-setting phase.** Early in a product or strategy, direction is uncertain and speed is secondary — you need to discover the right direction. Once direction is clear, speed matters enormously. Applying high-speed execution before direction is set burns resources; applying direction-setting deliberation in a validated fast-execution phase is competitive suicide.
- **Decompose velocity blockers into directional blockers and speed blockers.** "We're not moving fast enough" may be a capacity problem (speed blocker) or may be that the team is uncertain what to do (directional blocker). Each requires a different intervention.
- **Track velocity relative to the competitive environment.** Absolute velocity matters less than relative velocity — moving faster than the environment requires. A company moving quickly in a slow market is in a fine position; a company moving slowly in a fast-moving market is losing ground even if its absolute output is increasing.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Speed as a proxy for progress.** Shipping fast feels like velocity; shipping fast in the wrong direction is costly. Always test direction alongside speed.
- **Confusing directional clarity with certainty.** High velocity does not require knowing the full answer — it requires knowing the next best direction to move, and being willing to update it. Waiting for certainty is a speed blocker masquerading as directional care.
- **Acceleration without directional review.** As organizations grow, the capacity to move fast increases. Without periodic directional reviews, the increased speed goes in increasingly divergent directions across teams.

---

## Worked Example

A Series A startup is hiring quickly, shipping features at pace, and growing headcount. Six months later, they have doubled the team and have a much larger product. But DAU is flat. Velocity analysis: speed is high (lots of output), direction is questionable (the output isn't moving the metric that matters). The retrospective reveals the team was shipping features on a roadmap set eight months ago, before a significant market shift. Direction had been set correctly at the time; no mechanism existed to detect that the market had moved. The fix is a monthly directional check — does the roadmap still point toward the target? — not an acceleration of the existing roadmap.
