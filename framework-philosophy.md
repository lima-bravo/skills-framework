# Framework Philosophy
## What this is, what earns a card, and what stays out

---

## What this framework is for

This is a library of mental models for disciplined critical thinking — designed for practitioners who need to reason well under pressure, make better decisions, and structure complex problems. The target reader is a senior knowledge worker: a consultant, strategist, product leader, or innovation professional operating at the intersection of analysis, judgment, and execution.

The framework does not teach facts or domain knowledge. It teaches *how to think* — patterns of reasoning that apply across domains, situations, and levels of seniority. A card on First Principles is as useful to a product manager deciding what to build as to a CEO deciding whether to exit a market. Universality is a selection criterion.

The practical test: a well-internalized card changes how you approach a class of problem, not just one specific problem. If a concept only applies in one narrow context, it belongs in a domain handbook, not here.

---

## What earns a card

A concept earns a card if it satisfies all four of the following:

**1. It is a transferable thinking pattern, not a domain fact.**
The card must describe a way of structuring thought or a pattern of reasoning — not a piece of domain-specific knowledge. *Compounding* earns a card because the logic of exponential accumulation applies to financial returns, skills, reputation, and technical debt equally. The specific mechanics of compound interest in a savings account do not earn a card.

**2. It has genuine predictive or diagnostic power.**
The concept must help a practitioner anticipate what will happen, diagnose what is happening, or decide what to do. Models that merely describe without enabling prediction or diagnosis are excluded. A concept that only generates interesting conversation but doesn't change decisions belongs in a reading list, not a framework.

**3. It has a named failure mode that practitioners actually fall into.**
Every card must have a Failure Modes section that describes specific, realistic ways the model is misapplied or ignored. If a concept has no real failure mode — if its application is always obviously correct — it is probably either trivial or so broad as to be meaningless. The failure mode test also guards against including concepts that are purely aspirational ("be curious," "think long-term").

**4. It is distinct from existing cards.**
The framework actively resists redundancy. Before a card is written, the question must be asked: is this a genuinely distinct concept, or is it a restatement of something already in the library? Where two concepts overlap substantially, the card must explicitly name the distinction and link to the related card. Partial overlap is acceptable and normal; duplication of the core insight is not.

## What is deliberately excluded

**Personality and trait models.**
Myers-Briggs, DISC, Big Five, and similar frameworks describe *who people are* rather than *how to think*. They belong in HR and coaching contexts, not in a decision-making library. The framework covers what people do (incentives, bias, group dynamics) but not what they inherently are.

**Process compliance frameworks.**
ISO standards, ITIL, PRINCE2, specific methodology bodies of knowledge — these are compliance frameworks, not thinking tools. The framework includes the underlying principles that make processes work (flow, feedback loops, empirical control) but not the procedural scaffolding of any specific methodology. Agile thinking earns cards; the Scrum Guide does not.

**Domain-specific technical knowledge.**
Valuation models (DCF, comparables), clinical protocols, engineering specifications — these require domain expertise to apply correctly and are only transferable within their domain. The framework covers the underlying logic (Compounding for any exponential growth context; Reference Class Forecasting for any estimation problem) but not the domain-specific instantiation.

**Motivational and self-help content.**
Concepts that are primarily prescriptions to behave better rather than analytical tools for thinking more clearly. "Be resilient," "embrace failure," "growth mindset" in their popular forms are excluded. The framework covers the psychological mechanisms behind resilience and failure response (Loss Aversion, Commitment & Consistency Bias, After-Action Review) but not the motivational framing.

**Concepts fully captured by an existing card's Connections section.**
If a concept is adequately described as a special case, complement, or component of an existing card, it does not get its own card. It gets a named mention in the Connections section of the card it belongs to.

## Organising principles

The 16 categories are not arbitrary. They reflect the major domains from which transferable mental models are drawn — not the domains *to which* they are applied. A model that originates in physics (Activation Energy) applies to organisational change; it lives in Physics & Chemistry because that is where the underlying logic comes from, and where practitioners can find the analogical reasoning most clearly.

The categories are designed to be:
- **Mutually exclusive at the model level** — each card belongs to one category
- **Collectively sufficient at the domain level** — the category set covers the major intellectual traditions from which useful thinking models are drawn
- **Stable under growth** — new cards are added to existing categories; new categories are added only when a body of models exists that cannot be accommodated within the current 16

The exception is **Pre-built Chains**, which is not a source domain but an application layer — multi-model sequences designed for specific recurring decision types. Chains are written only when a situation recurs frequently enough to warrant a prescribed sequence, and when that sequence materially outperforms applying models ad hoc.

## How the framework evolves

**Cards are added when a genuine gap is identified.**
A gap exists when practitioners repeatedly face a class of problem that no existing card addresses, or where the existing cards are being stretched to cover territory they were not designed for. Gap identification is the primary driver of new cards; the framework does not grow by systematic enumeration of all possible models.

**Cards are not removed; they are revised.**
A card that has become stale, superseded, or misunderstood is updated, not deleted. The connection graph and situation-finder provide navigational filters; a card that is rarely surfaced will naturally recede without being removed.

**The selection logic is the constraint.**
When in doubt about whether a concept earns a card, the four criteria above are the test. A card proposed for reasons of completeness ("we should probably have something on X") rather than genuine practitioner need almost always fails at least one criterion.

**Quality over quantity.**
The framework is useful in proportion to the quality of each card, not the number of cards. A card that accurately diagnoses a failure mode is more valuable than three cards that describe correct behaviour in general terms. The target for each card is: a practitioner who reads it should leave knowing something they can act on that they did not know before.

---

## What the framework is not

It is not a curriculum. The cards are not sequenced for learning from scratch; they are a reference library for practitioners who already operate in complex environments and need better tools.

It is not a competency model. There are no levels, certifications, or assessments. The framework does not evaluate the reader; it offers the reader tools for evaluating situations.

It is not complete. No framework of this kind can be complete, and claiming completeness would be a failure mode. The goal is sufficient coverage of the thinking patterns that recur most in the target practitioner's environment — not exhaustive coverage of all possible mental models.

It is not static. The framework reflects current understanding of what earns a card. That understanding should itself be challenged, revised, and improved over time.
