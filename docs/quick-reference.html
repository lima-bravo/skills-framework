Skills Framework — How to Use

Card anatomy · 10-minute engagement · Key chains · High-signal taglines
Card anatomy — five sections
  • DefinitionWhat the concept is and where it comes from. Skip if familiar.
  • Mental ModelRead first. The core insight in one paragraph.
  • HeuristicsRead second. "When you see X, do Y" — skim for the one that fits.
  • Failure ModesRead before acting. How smart people misapply this model.
  • Worked ExampleConcrete case. Look for structural parallels, not surface similarity.
  • ConnectionsLinks to related models. Use for chaining once the card is internalized.
10-minute engagement
  1. Read the Mental Model paragraph. Compress it to one sentence.
  2. Read the Heuristics. Identify the one most relevant to your situation.
  3. Read the Failure Modes. Ask: am I already in one?
  4. Write 2–3 sentences on what this reveals about your situation. Reading produces recognition; writing produces understanding.

High-signal taglines — the core insight in one sentence
  • Drift to FailureSystems don't suddenly fail — they migrate there, one reasonable decision at a time.
  • Goodhart's LawWhen a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.
  • Survivorship BiasYou only see the winners — the losers have already left the room.
  • Hindsight Bias"I knew it all along" — the three most expensive words in a post-mortem.
  • Fundamental Attribution ErrorTheir failure is character; yours is circumstance.
  • Narrative InstinctWe don't record events — we compose them into stories, and stories lie.
  • FalsificationA belief you can't break isn't knowledge — it's faith.
  • Base Rate NeglectThe vivid case in front of you is not more informative than the thousand cases behind it.
  • Commitment & Consistency BiasOnce we've taken a position, we defend it — not because it's right, but because it's ours.
  • SRK FrameworkExperts don't think harder — they think less. Until the situation demands otherwise.
  • Social ProofWhen uncertain, people look sideways to decide what to do.
  • Cynefin FrameworkMatch your decision method to the domain — clear, complicated, complex, or chaotic.
  • Mission CommandCommunicate intent, not method — then trust the person closest to the problem to decide.
  • After-Action ReviewFour questions, asked immediately after, by the people who did the work.
  • Tactical PauseA deliberate halt mid-execution to stop, gather, think, then move — takes minutes, saves weeks.
  • Principal–Agent ProblemWhen you delegate, the person acting on your behalf has different information and different incentives.
  • OODA LoopObserve, Orient, Decide, Act — win by cycling faster than your environment changes.
  • Leader's IntentA three-part statement of purpose, key tasks, and end state that guides judgment when the plan no longer fits.
  • Wardley MappingA map that shows not just where things are, but which direction they're moving.
  • CalibrationBeing right on any single call is luck. Being right at the rate you predicted is skill.
  • Observer BiasThe evaluator who knows what they're looking for will find it.
  • Empirical Process ControlIn complex work, the process that produces good outcomes is one that continuously corrects itself.
  • Crawl / Walk / RunEach stage earns the right to the next. Failing the gate is not failure — running past it is.
  • Unit EconomicsThe business model works in the boardroom. The question is whether it works per customer.
  • Value Creation and CaptureCreating value and keeping some of it are two different problems.
  • Business Case ConstructionA business case that can only survive friendly scrutiny is not a business case.
  • Pricing LogicCost-plus pricing tells the customer what you paid. Value-based pricing asks what it's worth to them.
  • Power MappingThe org chart shows who is accountable. The power map shows who decides.
  • Influence Without AuthorityPositional power tells people what to do. Influence makes them want to.
  • Coalition BuildingAn idea with one champion is fragile. An idea with a coalition is a movement.
  • SteelmanningBuild the strongest possible version of the opposing view before engaging it.
  • Red TeamingMandate a team to attack your plan before your adversary does.
  • A/B TestingLet reality choose between two versions — before you commit to either.
  • Grice's MaximsWhat you include — and what you leave out — signals as much as the words themselves.
  • Expected ValueMultiply each outcome by its probability. Sum. That's what the decision is actually worth.
  • Bayesian UpdatingRevise your belief by the amount the evidence distinguishes the hypotheses — no more, no less.
  • Power LawA small number of inputs produce a disproportionate share of outputs — and the average hides it.
  • Black SwanThe event that dominates outcomes is precisely the one your model excluded.
  • Nash EquilibriumStable doesn't mean good. The equilibrium everyone is heading for may be the worst collective outcome.
  • Prisoner's DilemmaBoth players defect. Both are worse off. Both are being completely rational.
  • Tragedy of the CommonsNo individual does anything irrational. The shared resource collapses anyway.
  • Cobra EffectThe intervention that funds the problem it was designed to eliminate.
  • Parkinson's LawWork expands to fill the time available — and organisations expand to generate the work that fills them.
  • Peter PrincipleIn a hierarchy, every position tends to be filled by someone incompetent to execute it.
  • Dunbar's NumberAt ~150, relationships become statistics — and the coordination model must change accordingly.
  • Path DependenceWhere you can go depends on where you have been — and the cost of the detour compounds.
  • Overton WindowThe right answer in the wrong window doesn't get adopted. Shift the window before making the ask.
  • Fermi EstimationDecompose the unknowable into factors you can estimate — then triangulate two approaches.
  • Funnel IntakeAn unenriched idea is a bet placed before the odds are known.
  • Fixes That FailThe symptomatic fix works. That's the problem.
  • System ArchetypesSame structure, same trouble — recognise the pattern, find the leverage.
  • Requisite VarietyOnly variety absorbs variety — amplify yours or attenuate theirs.
  • CATWOEDefine the system — and whose worldview it serves — before you redesign it.
  • Playing to WinFive choices. One cascade. Each constrains and enables the next.
  • Technical DebtEvery shortcut you take today is a tax on every delivery tomorrow.
  • Reliability vs Feature VelocityMake the reliability trade-off explicit — or the system will make it for you.
  • Span of ControlCommunication links grow as n(n−1)/2. Add people; multiply coordination cost.
  • Coaching StanceThe expert who always has the answer leaves behind a client who can't function without them.
  • FacilitationThe room's intelligence is only as good as the structure you give it.
  • Systems ThinkingSee the whole before fixing the parts — the behavior you can't explain is the structure you haven't mapped.
  • Incentive DesignThe behavior you get is the behavior you designed for — whether you meant to or not.
  • AI Tool TeardownThe model is the interchangeable part; the platform is everything else.
